The tools

1. ResearchCube browser · made by us

Best for: a thesis or paper where your sources, PDFs and LaTeX belong together.

Strengths

  • LaTeX compiles inside your browser, so there is no server-side compile timeout.
  • 2 collaborators per project, real-time editing, comments and suggested changes (track changes) on the free plan.
  • Built-in reference library (one click, DOI or .bib), PDF reader with highlights, and AI search over 250M+ works.
  • IEEE, Elsevier, Springer, ACM, REVTeX, AMS and thesis templates, including İTÜ and TÜBİTAK.
  • Your files stay only in your own Google Drive.

Limits on the free plan

  • 5 projects; 5 AI requests and 5 literature searches a day.
  • AI uses your own free Gemini key.
  • Sign-in with a Google account only; no Git integration.

2. Papeeria browser

Best for: writing with many co-authors on one free project.

Strengths

  • Free “Epsilon” plan with unlimited collaborators per project.
  • Unlimited public projects and public templates.
  • Git sync with public repositories on the free plan.
  • Download projects as ZIP to work offline with a local TeX distribution.

Limits on the free plan

  • One active private project.
  • File edit history covers the last 24 hours.
  • Private Git repositories on the paid plan.

Source: papeeria.com/about/pricing

3. TeXPage browser

Best for: a familiar Overleaf-like editor with a longer free compile limit.

Strengths

  • 30-second compile time limit per project on the free plan.
  • Online LaTeX editing in the browser; real-time collaboration on its paid plans.

Limits on the free plan

  • 1 collaborator per project.
  • 2 saved project versions.

Source: texpage.com/pricing

4. Typst browser · open-source compiler

Best for: a new document where you do not have to use LaTeX.

Strengths

  • Free web app: create, share and collaborate on projects.
  • Community packages and templates; converts files from LaTeX, Word and Markdown.
  • The compiler is open source and runs on your own computer too.

Limits on the free plan

  • Typst is its own markup language, not LaTeX: journal LaTeX templates do not apply directly.
  • 200 MB storage, up to 100 files per project.
  • GitHub/GitLab sync and comments on the paid plan.

Source: typst.app/pricing

5. TeXstudio desktop · open source (GPL v2)

Best for: working offline with a full-featured LaTeX editor.

Strengths

  • Free and open source; runs on Windows, Linux, BSD and macOS.
  • No compile limits beyond your own computer.
  • Collaborative editing with teamtype (macOS and Linux at the time of writing).

Keep in mind

  • Needs a TeX distribution installed on your computer (such as TeX Live or MiKTeX).
  • No built-in cloud sync; share files yourself.

Source: texstudio.org

6. VS Code + LaTeX Workshop desktop · open source (MIT)

Best for: people who already write code in VS Code.

Strengths

  • Free extension for a popular editor, with PDF preview and build tools.
  • Works with Git and the rest of the VS Code ecosystem.

Keep in mind

  • Needs a LaTeX distribution on your system (TeX Live is recommended).
  • Collaborative editing through Live Share is experimental.

Source: github.com/James-Yu/LaTeX-Workshop

7. LyX desktop · open source (GPL v2+)

Best for: writing by structure without typing LaTeX commands.

Strengths

  • A “what you see is what you mean” document processor built on LaTeX.
  • Free and open source; runs on Linux, Windows and macOS.

Keep in mind

  • A different way of working from raw LaTeX; co-authors need LyX too.
  • No real-time collaboration.

Source: lyx.org

How to choose

  • Many co-authors, free: Papeeria.
  • Sources, PDFs and LaTeX in one place, with track changes for free: ResearchCube.
  • No need for LaTeX, starting fresh: Typst.
  • Offline or very large documents: TeXstudio or VS Code with LaTeX Workshop.
  • You prefer not to write LaTeX commands: LyX.
  • Your institution pays for Overleaf: staying there may be simplest.

Questions

What is the best free Overleaf alternative?

It depends on what you need. For many co-authors on one free project, Papeeria; for LaTeX together with a reference library, PDF reader and track changes, ResearchCube; for a new document without LaTeX, Typst; for working offline, TeXstudio, VS Code with LaTeX Workshop or LyX.

Which free LaTeX editor has the fewest compile limits?

Desktop editors (TeXstudio, VS Code with LaTeX Workshop, LyX) compile with the TeX distribution on your computer. Among online editors, ResearchCube compiles inside your browser, so there is no server-side time limit; TeXPage's free plan allows 30 seconds per compile.

Can I move an Overleaf project to another editor?

Yes. Overleaf lets you download a project's source as a .zip. Online editors such as ResearchCube, Papeeria and TeXPage accept the .tex files and figures; desktop editors open them directly.
